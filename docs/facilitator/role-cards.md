# Role Cards

Print these or copy them into team channels.

## Cowork teams

In Cowork, everyone builds their own twin. These roles rotate inside the working session. When the team is improving one person's twin, use the cards below. Then switch.

---

## The Subject

**Your job**

Bring the real behavior. Do not perform your values. Tell the team what you actually did last time.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Pick one recent priority conflict.
- Describe what happened.
- Name the people affected.
- Say what you did, not what you think you should have done.

**What good looks like**

The team can write one clear decision rule from your story: "When X conflicts with Y, choose Z unless..."

---

## The Interviewer

**Your job**

Extract behavior. Keep asking for the last real example.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Ask "When did this last happen?"
- Ask "What did you do next?"
- Ask "Who was disappointed?"
- Ask "What did you trade off?"

**What good looks like**

The subject stops speaking in abstractions and starts naming actual actions, constraints, and tiebreakers.

---

## The Voice Analyst

**Your job**

Turn sent mail into voice rules.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Read 5–10 sent emails.
- Mark openers, signoffs, punctuation tells, sentence length, and directness.
- Split rules by recipient type: manager, peer, exec, customer, broad audience.

**What good looks like**

`voice.md` includes real samples plus rules the samples prove. Not vibes. Evidence.

---

## The Builder

**Your job**

Keep the skill runnable.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Create or update `SKILL.md`.
- Put `soul.md`, `voice.md`, and `revealed.md` in `references/`.
- Run one fresh prompt through the twin.

**What good looks like**

The same files can run in Cowork, VS Code Copilot, and the GitHub Copilot CLI without rewriting the spec.

---

## The Skeptic

**Your job**

Find the lie, the overfit, and the interference.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Challenge any rule that sounds aspirational.
- Ask for the observed example behind it.
- Re-test after each patch.
- Watch for one word changing unrelated behavior.

**What good looks like**

The twin gets more specific without getting longer, softer, or more fragile.

---

## Code teams: council owners

Code teams build one integrated system. Each person owns one council agent. All agents share one `soul.md` and must integrate.

---

## Ambition owner

**Your job**

Represent goals, growth, strategic bets, and upside.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Identify the objective the person would be proud to advance.
- Name the opportunity cost of playing too safe.
- Write the ambition rule in one sentence.

**What good looks like**

The council can explain what future value is worth protecting.

---

## Obligation owner

**Your job**

Represent commitments, stakeholders, promises, and trust.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- List active promises.
- Rank stakeholders by responsibility, not seniority alone.
- Write the obligation rule in one sentence.

**What good looks like**

The council does not drop peer promises just because an exec appears.

---

## Capacity owner

**Your job**

Represent time, energy, meeting load, and realistic throughput.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Read the calendar pattern.
- Identify overload signals.
- Write the capacity boundary in one sentence.

**What good looks like**

The council recommends work the person could actually do.

---

## Arbiter owner

**Your job**

Make the final call when good principles collide.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Collect the council's rules.
- Turn conflicts into explicit tiebreakers.
- Decide what happens when rules disagree.

**What good looks like**

The system makes a clear recommendation and can explain why.

---

## Critic owner

**Your job**

Find failure modes before the human sees the draft.

**Your first 10 minutes**

- Attack the recommendation.
- Look for missing stakeholders.
- Look for vague language, passive decisions, and invented facts.

**What good looks like**

The final output is safer, sharper, and still recognizably tied to the shared `soul.md`.
