# Interview Script

Use this to extract decision rules for `soul.md`.

## Why behavioral

When we built one of these ourselves, the first version was worse than useless — because the author wrote down who they *wanted* to be. Their spec said they escalated priority conflicts; in reality, they just decided. It said they'd never ship unverified work; in reality, they cut scope and held the date. Rewriting those rules from what they actually did was what made the twin recognizable.

So don't ask, "What do you value?" Ask, "What did you actually do last time?"

## Behavioral questions

1. Think of the last time two important people needed you at once. What did you actually do first?
2. When did you last miss or renegotiate a commitment? What did you say, and to whom?
3. Tell me about the last time you shipped something imperfect. What did you cut, keep, or flag?
4. When did you last push back on a senior person? What words did you use?
5. When did you last protect a peer's work from churn? What action did you take?
6. Think of the last surprise request that landed late in the day. Did you absorb it, defer it, delegate it, or decline it?
7. When did your calendar last contradict your stated priorities? What won?
8. Tell me about the last decision you made without escalating. Why did you decide instead of asking?
9. When did you last escalate? What made that situation different?
10. What was the last message you rewrote three times before sending? What were you trying not to say?
11. When did you last choose speed over completeness? What risk did you accept?
12. When did you last choose completeness over speed? What deadline or relationship paid the cost?
13. Tell me about the last meeting you skipped, shortened, or protected. What did that reveal?
14. When did you last disappoint someone intentionally? Why was that the least-bad option?
15. What is the last piece of feedback you ignored? What did you do instead?

::: tip Interviewer move
If the answer starts with "I usually" or "I value," interrupt kindly: "Great. What happened the last time?"
:::

## Forced-choice dilemmas

Have participants answer these at the start and seal their answers. At the end, the twin answers cold. Compare for correction, not points.

| # | Dilemma | Choices | Probes |
| - | ------- | ------- | ------ |
| 1 | A deck ships Friday. Thursday afternoon you find an unverified claim on slide 12. | A) Ship it and flag verbally B) Cut the slide and ship on time C) Slip the date D) Escalate | Quality bar, date discipline, escalation rule |
| 2 | Your manager asks for a quick narrative by EOD. You promised a peer a review by EOD. | A) Do manager first B) Do peer first C) Split both shallowly D) Renegotiate one commitment | Stakeholder table, promise handling |
| 3 | An exec asks for a meeting during your only focus block this week. | A) Accept B) Offer async input C) Ask for delegate coverage D) Decline with context | Capacity, seniority pressure, boundaries |
| 4 | A customer-facing email is accurate but sounds cold. It is due in 20 minutes. | A) Send as is B) Rewrite for tone C) Ask someone to review D) Delay | Voice bar, risk tolerance, audience handling |
| 5 | A teammate proposes an approach you think will fail, but the team is tired. | A) Object now B) Let it run C) Ask one diagnostic question D) Take it offline | Candor, team energy, conflict style |
| 6 | You have data that weakens your preferred recommendation. | A) Include it prominently B) Mention it in appendix C) Hold it for Q&A D) Change recommendation | Evidence handling, integrity boundary |
| 7 | Two teams disagree and both expect you to choose today. | A) Decide B) Escalate C) Facilitate one more discussion D) Defer | Arbiter behavior, escalation threshold |
| 8 | Your calendar is over capacity and a non-urgent meeting has no agenda. | A) Attend B) Decline C) Ask for agenda D) Send async notes | Capacity, meeting boundaries |
| 9 | A draft from AI is 80% right and 20% not your voice. | A) Rewrite fully B) Patch the worst parts C) Send with light edits D) Start over manually | Voice standards, efficiency threshold |
| 10 | A peer asks for confidential context you are not sure you can share. | A) Share high level B) Decline C) Ask owner first D) Redirect to source | Boundaries, trust, confidentiality |
| 11 | You discover a dependency risk that might not materialize. | A) Raise now B) Monitor quietly C) Add a caveat D) Build a fallback | Risk signaling, ambiguity tolerance |
| 12 | A project can hit the date only if scope shrinks. | A) Cut scope B) Slip date C) Add people D) Escalate tradeoff | Priority stack, scope/date tiebreaker |
| 13 | A broad update could be short and clear or nuanced and long. | A) Short B) Nuanced C) Two-layer summary D) Ask audience preference | Communication style, audience model |
| 14 | You get feedback that is partly wrong but politically important. | A) Accept it B) Push back C) Incorporate the useful part D) Ask a third party | Feedback handling, stakeholder sensitivity |
| 15 | Your twin recommends something that sounds unlike you but is logically defensible. | A) Keep it B) Patch voice C) Patch decision rule D) Add a boundary | Test behavior, correction strategy |
