# Scenario 2 — The Greenlight

::: tip Alternate take on Scenario 2
This is the **council** take on Content Health — a room of audiences that scores, debates, and decides what to build. The **lens** take (*The Critic*) solves the same slide-9 need with a single audience reviewer. **Pick one. They are separate.**
:::

## The problem (villain's complaint)

> Our agents write content. Our evals check whether it's accurate, clear, and roughly the right level — and they pass almost everything. They review with **one implied reader**, an average of everyone that exists nowhere. So competent content aimed at the wrong reader passes every time. Four blind passes of the provided reviewer over five articles: not one flagged the worst piece as a reject — a governance whitepaper filed against a store lead. One verdict can't say *"ship it for Compliance, reject it for Retail,"* so it split the difference and missed.

## What 'done' looks like

> A **council** of audience personas that takes a subject and scores it from every seat at once — each seat judging against *its own outcome*, each score carrying a quote, a source, and a confidence. The room debates where the verdicts split, then derives a **transformation plan**: which asset, in which format, for whom — including *"this shouldn't be a document."* Finally it **greenlights** the plan by re-scoring it against the same seats. Nothing ships until the audience that rejected the original would pass its replacement. It runs without you.

## The core mechanic — two passes, one rubric

- **Pass 1 · `convene`** — every seat scores the subject. The verdicts diverge on purpose.
- **The debate** — the room surfaces the conflicts, evidence-bound.
- **The plan** — the failures become a build spec: per-audience assets, honest format calls.
- **Pass 2 · `greenlight`** — re-score the plan on the *same* criteria. The council that vetoes is the council that clears.

## The data

Everything ships in the kit. **The data pack is shared with the other Scenario 2 take.**

| Folder | What's in it |
|---|---|
| `the-greenlight/` | The provided skill. Seats the council, convenes it, runs the debate, greenlights the plan. |
| `data-pack/content/` | Five articles (learn unit, how-to, blog, exec summary, quickstart), all about the same capability, each declaring who it was written for |
| `data-pack/audience-cards/` | Four worked audience cards — the seats of the council |
| `data-pack/style-guide/` | The house style rules |

**Your job:** seat two or more audiences who want *different outcomes*, convene them over a piece, and show where they disagree — then turn that disagreement into a plan the room will greenlight.

> **You never change the solo critic.** It's the one-verdict "before" you're measuring the council against.

## The two rules

| | Rule |
|:---:|---|
| 🎯 | **A criterion is a bar that protects one audience's outcome** — not "is it good?", but "will this get *my* people to the result they need?" |
| 🧾 | **No claim without evidence, a source, and a confidence** — every score quotes the text, names what backs it, and rates its own certainty. Low confidence on a fatal criterion abstains. |

## Two beats everyone hits

### Team checkpoint · ~10 minutes, around halfway

Stop building. Talk to your table.

| | |
|:---:|---|
| 1 | Read out **one conflict** — a passage two seats scored in opposite directions |
| 2 | Read out **one seat whose criteria aren't earning their place** (they'd score the same for any audience) |
| 3 | **Steal the sharpest seat you heard.** Reseat it for your own audience — don't copy it straight. |
| 4 | Agree in one sentence: what makes two seats *genuinely* disagree? |

If two seats at your table never disagree, at least one of them isn't reading its card.

### The twist · ~55 minutes in

**One of your audiences is about to change.** The facilitator announces it; you apply it to that seat and re-convene.

It won't be a new objective — it's pressure on the room you already have. Something like *"Retail now gets 90 seconds instead of six minutes,"* or *"Compliance can no longer accept any unsourced claim,"* or *"half of Manufacturing is on night shift with nobody to ask."*

Change that one line on the seat. Re-convene all five pieces.

**If the verdicts barely move, that seat was never really judging for its own outcome** — it's been scoring generic quality in costume. That's the most useful thing you'll learn today.
