# The Dispatch — land one routing decision

## Turn the positions into one decision the room owns

Every team has taken a position (`convene.md`). Now the room lands **one routing decision**. Don't re-litigate the positions — build the decision *from* them.

**Who fields it is often the easy part** — the team most "in," or the one whose charter fits best. **The debate is the plan** — the deliverables, the audience, and the reuse. Spend the room's energy there.

---

## What to do, in order

### 1. Decide the owner — who fields it
Pick exactly **one primary team** to field and coordinate the request — usually the team most "in," whose charter fits best. Co-owners are fine, but name the lead. This is often quick; don't over-debate it.

### 2. Decide the audience — who it's *really* for
The stated audience is a starting point, not the answer. If a team argued the real audience is different (Field & Partner: "it's partners, not customers"), weigh that. Name who it's really for — one or more. Never "everyone" without a primary.

### 3. Build the plan — the deliverables (this is the debate)
List the deliverables that satisfy the request. For each:

- **what** — the shape (a learning path, a workshop, a lab, an ASN journey, a credential…).
- **builder** — the one team that builds it.
- **reused_by** — the teams that reuse it (or empty). **Build once, reuse across teams** — never have two teams build the same thing.
- **audience** — who that deliverable serves.

This is where the room earns its keep. If DPO wants a live workshop and Content & Insights wants a path, the plan often is: *C&I builds the path once; DPO reuses it live; Field & Partner reuses it regionally.* One build, three teams served.

### 4. Pick the disposition

| Disposition | Use when |
|---|---|
| `proceed-as-is` | The ask is clear and one owner can run with it now. |
| `reshape` | The right move differs from the request's assumed shape or audience — repoint it. |
| `split` | It's genuinely two deliverables (e.g., an IT-pro track *and* an end-user track). |
| `defer` | Not enough to route yet, or no capacity/motion — park it with a condition to revisit. |
| `decline-and-redirect` | Not this room's to own — send it where it belongs, or back to the requester. |

### 5. Name the next action
One concrete next step with a team on it — "open a work item for C&I," "go back to the requester for the missing outcome," "brief Field & Partner." Not "TBD."

### 6. Check it against the guardrails
Before you call it done (these are in `../dispatch-data/policy/ROUTING-RULES.md`):

- **Routable first.** If the request was under-specified, the only honest dispositions are `defer` or `decline-and-redirect`.
- **One primary owner.** Zero owners, or two "primary" owners, is invalid.
- **Cover the audience.** Every audience you named is served by at least one deliverable.
- **Reuse is explicit.** Shared needs say *build once, reused by* — never built twice.
- **Certify only what's stable.** A credential deliverable needs stable objectives; if the topic churns, downgrade it or add a revisit condition.

## When the request isn't ready

If the request came in under-specified (a rough idea), don't force a decision. Land a `defer` or `decline-and-redirect`, and say exactly what to sharpen:

> **Disposition:** defer — not routable yet.
> **What's missing:** a desired outcome and a real audience ("sellers" — which sellers?).
> **Next action:** go back to the requester for the outcome and audience, then re-dispatch.

That's not a failure — catching an under-specified request *before* the room routes it is the honest thing the exercise teaches.

---

## A few rules

- **Build the decision from the positions.** Don't re-run the room.
- **Owner is quick; the plan is the work.** Put the debate on deliverables and reuse.
- **Build once, reuse across teams.** The reuse is the insight; make it explicit in the plan.
- **A not-routable request is deferred, not routed.** Say what to sharpen.
- **Name a real next action** — a concrete step with a team on it.
