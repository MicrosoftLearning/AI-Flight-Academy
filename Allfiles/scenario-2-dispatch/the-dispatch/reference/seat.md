# Seat a team

## Add a team and what makes it say yes or no

You're helping the participant add one team to `THE-ROOM.md`: its name, what it owns, who it serves, and what makes it want a request or pass it on. Do this one team at a time, and push each "says yes / says no" until it's specific to *this* team.

Think of it as filling a room — and the room only works if the teams want different things.

---

## Before you start

**Use Work IQ first if it's available.** A real team the participant actually works with beats a made-up one. Pull what the org data already knows — the team's charter, who it serves, what it ships — then ask only what it can't tell you. Say which parts you found and which you're guessing. The team cards in `../dispatch-data/teams/` are a ready-made starting roster.

**Ask how many teams they want.** Two is the floor. **Three or four** is the sweet spot for a couple of hours — enough to disagree, few enough to follow.

---

## Start with the charter

Before anything else, get what the team owns and who it serves:

> "For this team, what are they accountable for, and who's their work for? That's what decides whether a request is theirs."

Everything else — what makes them say yes, their format bias — flows from that. Skip it and you'll get a team that wants everything, and the room won't disagree.

A few examples:

| Team | What makes it say yes |
|---|---|
| 📚 Content & Insights | durable, broad, certifiable — build once, reuse |
| 🎬 Delivery & Program Operations | a live audience with a date and a motion |
| 🌍 Field & Partner | the real audience is partners, sellers, or the field |
| 🛰️ Product (ASN) | it's a repeatable pattern, not a one-off |

---

## Then capture what makes it say yes or no

For each team, capture:

- **Says YES when** — the signals that make this team want to own a request.
- **Says NO / routes elsewhere when** — the signals that make it pass or repoint.
- **Format bias** — the shape this team pushes work into.
- **Voice** — how it argues; its opening move.

Write each into `THE-ROOM.md` using the block there.

---

## The test for every team

> Would this team route a request differently than another team in the room?
> - **Yes** — keep it. That's where the debate lives.
> - **No** — it's a duplicate of a team you already have. Change its charter, or drop it.

Push back once, kindly, when two teams feel the same:

> "These two would land the same plan. What does *this* team push for that the other wouldn't — a different audience, a different shape, a different appetite?"

Then move on. The clock matters.

---

## If they're stuck

| They say… | You do… |
|---|---|
| "I know our real teams" | Best case. Seat three with genuinely different charters. |
| "Give me some" | Seat three from the cards whose instincts clash — Content & Insights (build once), DPO (deliver live), and Field & Partner (repoint to partners) is the sharpest trio. |
| "They all seem similar" | Then you have one team, not a room. Change one hard: a platform team that says "don't build a one-off, build the system." Different instinct, different plan. |

---

## After adding

1. **Read the roster back** — the charters side by side. If two are basically the same, say so: the room won't disagree.
2. **Point at the contrast:** "These three want different things — dispatch RQ-01 and you'll see them split on the plan."
3. **Next:** dispatch a request, or add one more team first.

> A quick self-check: if you can't say in one sentence how two teams would route the *same* request *differently*, you haven't added two teams yet — you've added one twice.
