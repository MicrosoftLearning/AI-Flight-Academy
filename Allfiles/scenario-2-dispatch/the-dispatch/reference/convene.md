# Dispatch - every team takes a position

## Every team weighs in on the same request, then you compare

You're routing one request - something from `../dispatch-data/requests/`, or anything the participant pastes in (a formed brief, or a rough idea). Every team in `THE-ROOM.md` reacts to it by *its own* charter. Then you look at where they agree and where they don't.

This is the heart of the exercise. The result isn't one answer - it's one position per team, and the interesting part is where they differ, which is usually the plan.

---

## What to do, in order

### 1. Load the room
Read `THE-ROOM.md`. Get every team, what it owns, and what makes it say yes or no. If there are fewer than two teams, stop and say: "One team can't disagree with itself. Seat at least one more that wants something different - ask to seat the room."

### 2. Read the request - and check it's routable
Read the request. Does it say who it's for, the topic, and the desired outcome? If it's missing those - a rough idea - **don't route it.** Say what's missing and sharpen it first (pin the audience and outcome), then come back. A confident route on an under-specified request is exactly the miss to avoid.

### 3. Each team takes a position, one at a time
For each team, react to the request through *that team's* charter only. Do them independently - a later team shouldn't soften an earlier one. Each position carries:

- **An interest** - in (wants to own it), support (would help), or out (passes).
- **A deliverable** - what this team would make for it (drawn from what it produces), or nothing if it's out.
- **A reuse** - a deliverable it could reuse from another team, or one of its own another team could reuse. Often null; name it when it's there.
- **An effort** - S, M, or L.
- **A rationale** - one line in the team's voice, grounded in its card. No charter reason, no position.

### 4. Put it in a table

| Team | Interest | Would make | Reuse | One-line rationale |
|---|:---:|---|---|---|
| 📚 Content & Insights | in | governance learning path + credential | - | "Durable enough to build once and certify." |
| 🎬 DPO | support | live readiness workshop | reuse C&I's path | "Live pre-GA - but capacity's tight; I'd reuse the path." |
| 🌍 Field & Partner | in | partner activation | reuse the path, regionalized | "Partners *deploy* governance - they need it first." |

### 5. Talk through the disagreements
This is what makes it a room and not a stack of separate opinions. Find the real clashes - and notice they're usually about the **plan**, not who owns it.

> **The clash:** Content & Insights wants one evergreen path built once. DPO wants a live workshop now; Field & Partner says the real audience is partners, not customers.
> **What it shows:** the request isn't simply "route to team X" - it's a plan where one deliverable is built once and reused live and regionally. That reuse is the decision the room exists to make. (That's what `dispatch.md` lands next.)

Keep it grounded: every position cites the team's charter. A team doesn't get to just "want" a request - it argues from what it owns and serves.

### 6. Say who's in and who's out
Close with who wants the request and who passed:

> **In:** Content & Insights (owner), Field & Partner. **Support:** DPO. **Out:** MTTs (not technical enough), Product (too one-off).
> A single triager gives one answer. The room gives you a plan - and it names who builds and who reuses.

---

## Compare to the single-triager baseline

Always end by putting the room next to how one triager would route it (in `reference/solo.md`):

> **A lone triager said:** "Send it to Content & Insights." One answer, one imagined owner.
> **The room says:** C&I owns it, but the plan is a path built once and reused by DPO (live) and Field & Partner (regional), audience repointed to partners-first.
> **What the room saw that one triager couldn't:** the same request is different work to three teams - and the reuse is the point.

If every team lands the same plan, say so plainly - that's the thing to fix:

> Every team routed this the same way. Right now this is one triager in different hats. Either the teams aren't reasoning from their own charters, or this request is so obvious it doesn't need a room. Add a team that would repoint the audience or the shape.

---

## A few rules

- **Judge each team on its own charter.** Don't let one position bleed into another.
- **Interest, deliverable, rationale - every time.** Grounded in the card.
- **Name the disagreement; don't smooth it over.** The clash - usually about the plan and the reuse - is the useful part.
- **Don't invent a team's appetite.** If a team's card doesn't support wanting this, it passes.
- **A rough idea gets sharpened, not routed.** Missing audience/topic/outcome → say what's missing first.
- **Anything can be the request.** A pasted brief or a rough idea is fine.

---

## When to run it

- After seating or changing any team - this is the feedback loop.
- Start with **RQ-01** (agent governance before GA). It splits the room cleanly across owner, audience, and plan.
- Before you land the decision - the positions this turns up are what the decision is built from.
