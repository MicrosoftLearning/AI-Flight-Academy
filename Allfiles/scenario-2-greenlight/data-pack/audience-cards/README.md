# AUDIENCE CARDS
## Contoso Group · four business units, one rollout

**Read the card before you score the content.** Every piece in this data pack was written *for someone.* These cards are who. Content that is well-written but wrong for its audience is still wrong — and catching that is the point of the exercise.

*Fictional company, fictional roles, fictional product. See `data-pack/DISCLAIMER.md`.*

---

## The shared situation

| | |
|---|---|
| **Company** | Contoso Group — a diversified enterprise, ~78,000 employees across four business units |
| **The rollout** | **Microsoft 365 Copilot** went live enterprise-wide **five weeks ago**, with summarization in Teams as the lead capability |
| **Who wrote the content** | Contoso's central Enterprise Enablement team — one team, one capability, four very different audiences |
| **Why content fails here** | The team knows Copilot cold. They do not know what a plant floor, a trading desk, a nursing unit, and a store back room each make impossible. |

> **This is the realistic failure mode.** Enterprise content is usually written once and pushed to everyone. It is accurate, clear, well-formatted — and wrong for three out of four audiences.

## The four cards

| Card | Business unit | Role | The thing that breaks content for them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[AC-01](AC-01-retail-store-operations.md)** | 🛒 Contoso Retail | Store Operations Lead | No admin rights, no install, no budget — and 8 minutes, standing up |
| **[AC-02](AC-02-financial-compliance-risk.md)** | 🏦 Contoso Financial | Compliance & Risk Lead | Every claim is a supervisory record. Unsupported = a finding. |
| **[AC-03](AC-03-health-clinical-informatics.md)** | 🏥 Contoso Health | Clinical Informatics Manager | PHI, patient safety, and clinicians who will not read past line three |
| **[AC-04](AC-04-manufacturing-production.md)** | 🏭 Contoso Industries | Production Manager | The plant network is segmented. Half of what the content assumes cannot reach the line. |

### How they differ, at a glance

| | 🛒 **AC-01 Retail** | 🏦 **AC-02 Financial** | 🏥 **AC-03 Health** | 🏭 **AC-04 Manufacturing** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical depth** | None | Moderate | High | Moderate (OT, not IT) |
| **Regulatory exposure** | Low | **Extreme** | **Extreme** | High (safety) |
| **Can they decide alone?** | No — DM approval | Yes, and can **block** others | Shared with clinical governance | Yes, owns the line |
| **Reading context** | Standing, interrupted | At a desk, carefully, twice | Between patients | Shop floor, gloves, shift change |
| **Tolerance for a confident guess** | Medium | **Zero** | **Zero** | Low |
| **What "useful" means** | Saves me time today | Keeps me out of a finding | Doesn't put a patient at risk | Doesn't stop the line |

## Two things these cards deliberately do NOT tell you

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. How to score** | These are *facts* about the audience, not criteria. Turning them into something a reviewer applies consistently is your job — that's the assignment. |
| **2. What matters most** | Whether "they can't get admin rights" outranks "the tone talks down to them" is a judgment call. Make it, and defend it in your rubric. |

---

## Pairing rule

Each content piece declares which audience card it was written for.

> ### ⚠️ **Check that the declaration is true.**
> A piece that names AC-01 is not necessarily written for AC-01.

---

## A note on cross-audience content

A piece written for one unit is not automatically wrong for another — but it is rarely right. When you score, ask which card it was *actually* written for, then ask which card it *claims*.

The gap between those two is where most of this exercise lives.
