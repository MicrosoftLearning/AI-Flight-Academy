# Plan

## Turn the gaps into what to make instead – then check it

The review gave you a verdict from each audience, and usually a piece some of them reject. This mode does two things:

1. **Builds a plan** from those gaps: what to make, for whom, in what format.
2. **Checks the plan** against the same audiences. Nothing counts as done until the audiences that rejected the piece would accept what replaces it.

The plan isn't just advice – it has to pass the same room that asked for it.

---

## Step 1 – the gaps tell you what to make

Take the review. For every audience that said Revise or Reject, the needs that failed tell you what has to change – and often that the *format* is wrong.

For each under-served audience, write a short build order:

- **Audience** – which one.
- **Why it didn't work for them** – the need that failed, plus the quote from the review.
- **The honest format call** – if a 500-word write-up fails "usable on the floor," the answer isn't "trim it," it's "this shouldn't be a document."
- **What to make** – be concrete: "a 90-second video that opens with the one thing that changes," "a one-page job aid," "a three-line Teams message with the single action."
- **What it must include** – the two to four things this audience needs, straight from their profile.
- **What to leave out** – everything the other audiences needed that this one doesn't.

> **The format call is the point.** A general reviewer says "rewrite paragraph three." The room says "paragraph three is fine for Compliance and irrelevant to Retail – Retail doesn't need a better paragraph, they need a different thing." That's a call only a room of audiences can make. Make it out loud.

---

## Step 2 – the whole plan

Roll the build orders into one plan: the piece becomes a *set* of things, each aimed at an audience (or a few who want the same thing).

```
Plan · executive summary (P4)

Keep as-is → 🏦 Compliance      the detailed write-up already works for them
Make new   → 🛒 Retail          90-sec video: "what changes for your store, and the one thing to do"
Make new   → 🏭 Manufacturing   one-page job aid, readable offline at shift change
Left out   → (none)             every audience has something
```

If anyone's left out, say so. An audience with nothing in the plan is one you're choosing not to serve – that can be the right call, but it should be a decision, not an accident.

---

## Step 3 – check the plan

Now close the loop. For each new thing in the plan, review it against the *same audience's* needs – the description if it's not built yet, or the draft if the participant made one.

| Thing | For | Their earlier verdict | New verdict | Good to go? |
|---|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 90-sec video (described) | 🛒 Retail | Reject | Ship | yes |
| Job aid (described) | 🏭 Manufacturing | Reject | Ship | yes |
| Write-up (unchanged) | 🏦 Compliance | Ship | Ship | yes |

The rule: something is good to go only when the audience that rejected the original would accept its replacement, judged on the same needs, with the same quote-reason-confidence habit. The room that turned it down is the room that clears it.

If a new thing still doesn't pass, it goes back – the plan isn't finished. Say which need still fails and why.

---

## Step 4 – the call

Close with the room's decision:

> **Good to go.** The piece ships as a set: the write-up for Compliance, a 90-second video for Retail, a job aid for Manufacturing. Every audience is served. A single reviewer would have shipped one document to all three and lost two of them.

or

> **Not yet.** Retail's video is four minutes; their bar is about ninety seconds. Trim it or rethink it, then check again.

---

## How real this step is, by track

| Track | What the check is |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Cowork | The room re-reads the *description* of each new thing and confirms it would pass. Light and honest – you're checking the plan, not a finished asset. |
| 🔵 Studio | A helper drafts each thing; the room re-scores it. |
| 🟣 VS Code | Generate the thing (or an outline), re-run the check in code, and let the gate block until every audience passes. |

---

## A few rules

- **Build from the gaps, don't invent.** Every build order traces to a need that failed and its quote. No gap, no new thing.
- **Name the format call.** "Wrong format entirely" is the most useful thing you can say here. Don't shrink it to a copy edit.
- **Check on the same needs.** This step reuses the earlier needs, pointed at the replacement. That symmetry is what makes it mean something.
- **Leaving someone out is a decision.** Say it out loud.
