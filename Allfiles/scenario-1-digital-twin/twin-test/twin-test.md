# The Twin Test

**Do this before you build anything. It takes about four minutes.**

Fifteen forced-choice work situations. For each one, circle the letter that matches what you **actually did last time** — not what you'd like to have done.

Then fold it over and don't look at it again until the end of the session.

::: warning Answer fast
If you're deliberating, you're constructing an answer rather than recalling one. Aim for well under 30 seconds each. There are no good or bad answers here, only yours.
:::

At the end, your twin answers the same fifteen cold and you compare. **This is not a score.** The mismatches are the point — each one shows a rule you wrote down that doesn't match how you actually work.

---
**1.** A launch note is due Friday. Thursday afternoon you find one product claim you cannot verify.
(a) Ship it with a caveat &nbsp; (b) Cut the claim and ship on time &nbsp; (c) Slip the note until verified &nbsp; (d) Ask your manager to decide
› *probes: decision_rules — accuracy vs committed date*

**2.** Two peer teams ask for help this week and both say their request is urgent.
(a) Try to do both &nbsp; (b) Pick the higher-impact one yourself &nbsp; (c) Ask your manager to arbitrate &nbsp; (d) Tell the peers to align first
› *probes: decision_rules — priority conflict*

**3.** A three-hour all-hands lands on your only open focus block.
(a) Attend live &nbsp; (b) Mark tentative and half-watch &nbsp; (c) Decline and watch the recording &nbsp; (d) Ask whether attendance is required
› *probes: capacity — meeting load vs focus*

**4.** A teammate asks you to own one more workstream. You are already at capacity.
(a) Say yes &nbsp; (b) Say yes and quietly drop something else &nbsp; (c) Say yes only if a named item slips &nbsp; (d) Say no
› *probes: capacity — overcommitment rule*

**5.** You will miss a date you committed to. You realize it six days early.
(a) Tell the stakeholder now with a new real date &nbsp; (b) Ask for help before naming a date &nbsp; (c) Push hard and only report if you miss &nbsp; (d) Wait for the next 1:1
› *probes: voice — status update under pressure*

**6.** A senior person repeats an inaccurate claim in a group thread.
(a) Correct it publicly &nbsp; (b) DM them with the correction &nbsp; (c) Let it go &nbsp; (d) Correct it publicly but soften the wording
› *probes: boundaries — accuracy vs hierarchy*

**7.** A partner team sends a meeting invite titled "sync" with no agenda.
(a) Accept &nbsp; (b) Mark tentative &nbsp; (c) Ask for the agenda first &nbsp; (d) Offer to resolve async
› *probes: capacity — meeting intake rule*

**8.** You built a useful internal asset nobody asked for. Sharing it may create support work.
(a) Share broadly &nbsp; (b) Share with a no-support caveat &nbsp; (c) Keep it for yourself &nbsp; (d) Share only with your immediate team
› *probes: priority_stack — reusable leverage vs support cost*

**9.** A recurring 1:1 conflicts with a build block for the third time.
(a) Keep the 1:1 &nbsp; (b) Reschedule and offer two times &nbsp; (c) Cancel this occurrence &nbsp; (d) Shorten it to 15 minutes
› *probes: stakeholders — relationship maintenance*

**10.** An agent drafts a reply to your manager. It is factually right but does not sound like you.
(a) Send it &nbsp; (b) Rewrite from scratch &nbsp; (c) Edit the tone and send &nbsp; (d) Do not send; handle live
› *probes: voice — tolerable voice drift*

**11.** An external partner asks for a capability answer and you are only 80% sure.
(a) Answer with a caveat &nbsp; (b) Say you will confirm and follow up &nbsp; (c) Route them to the product owner &nbsp; (d) Give the answer confidently
› *probes: boundaries — external uncertainty*

**12.** You are heads-down and three important chats have waited two business days.
(a) Keep building and clear them tonight &nbsp; (b) Stop and clear all three now &nbsp; (c) Send a short delay note now &nbsp; (d) Clear only the most senior sender
› *probes: blind_spots — response latency under load*

**13.** A focus block you created is booked over by a non-urgent meeting.
(a) Let it go &nbsp; (b) Decline and cite the focus block &nbsp; (c) Mark tentative &nbsp; (d) Move the focus block
› *probes: boundaries — protecting focus time*

**14.** A necessary coordination task is adjacent to your role but has no clear owner.
(a) Own it and send the invite &nbsp; (b) Raise the gap and wait &nbsp; (c) Escalate the ownership gap &nbsp; (d) Do the minimum async without a meeting
› *probes: priority_stack — ownership of ambiguous work*

**15.** You are asked to commit to a date for content while the product is still changing weekly.
(a) Give the requested date &nbsp; (b) Give a date with explicit assumptions &nbsp; (c) Give a range &nbsp; (d) Refuse to commit until stable
› *probes: decision_rules — dates under uncertainty*

## At the end of the session

Ask your twin to answer all fifteen, cold:

```text
Using my twin, answer these fifteen dilemmas as I would actually answer them —
not as I would like to, and not as a well-adjusted professional would.
Reply with just the numbers and letters.
```

Then fill in the bottom two rows. For every mismatch, look at the field named under that dilemma — that's the part of your soul file to fix.

The interesting misses are where **the twin picked the sensible answer and you wouldn't have.**
